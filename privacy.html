Privacy

Last changed: 20 November 2026

Fermi Frog is a small daily game made by Eliott Montagnon, who is responsible for the data described here. Questions or requests: eliott@montagnon.org.

In short: no ads, no tracking, nothing sold. You can play without giving anything, and what you choose to share can be deleted.

If you just play

Your frog, your settings and your rounds are kept in your own browser (its “local storage”). They do not leave your device unless you post a score or sign in. Clearing your browser's site data erases them.

If you post a score

The leaderboard keeps the name you type, the frog you picked, the points of your five answers, the time, and a random number that tells your scores apart from other players'. The name and frog are shown publicly on that day's board. Do not use your real name if you prefer not to. To have a score removed, write to the address above.

If you sign in with Google (optional)

Signing in only serves to keep your frog, settings and rounds on all your devices.

  • Google tells us one thing: an account number that it makes for this game. We do not receive your email, your name or your contacts.
  • We keep, next to that number, the same saved data your browser keeps (frog, settings, rounds), in one private file.
  • A cookie keeps you signed in for up to 180 days. It is used for nothing else. Another short cookie (10 minutes) protects the sign-in itself.
  • “Sign out” forgets this browser. “Delete my data”, in the profile, deletes the frog, the settings and the rounds at once. If you bought the supporter plan, the account number and the record of the purchase are kept, so that you do not lose what you paid for.

We use this data because you asked for it to be kept (your consent, which you can withdraw by deleting the account).

If you buy the supporter plan (optional)

You pay on a page run by Stripe, a payment company. Your card and the email you type there go to Stripe, not to us: we never see them. Next to your account number we keep three things: that the plan is paid, when, and Stripe's reference for the payment.

Who else is involved

  • Vercel hosts the game and stores the scores and accounts, in its Paris region. Like any web host, it sees your IP address when your browser asks for a page.
  • Google Fonts supplies the game's lettering, so Google sees your IP address when the page loads.
  • Google handles signing in, if you choose to.
  • Stripe takes the payment, if you buy the supporter plan.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete what we hold about you, by writing to the address above. If you think your data is mishandled, you can also complain to your data protection authority (in France, the CNIL: cnil.fr).
